Association football, also known as soccer, is the world's most popular sport with an estimated 4 billion fans globally.
The FIFA World Cup is held every four years and is the most prestigious tournament in football.
Uruguay hosted and won the first FIFA World Cup in 1930.
Brazil has won the FIFA World Cup a record five times: 1958, 1962, 1970, 1994, and 2002.
A standard football match consists of two 45-minute halves, for a total of 90 minutes.
Each football team fields eleven players on the pitch, including one goalkeeper.
The offside rule prevents an attacking player from being closer to the opponent's goal line than both the ball and the second-to-last defender when the ball is played to them.
Pele, from Brazil, is the only player to win three FIFA World Cups, in 1958, 1962, and 1970.
Diego Maradona famously scored the "Hand of God" goal for Argentina in the 1986 World Cup quarter-final against England.
Lionel Messi won the FIFA World Cup with Argentina in 2022 in Qatar.
Cristiano Ronaldo and Lionel Messi are widely regarded as two of the greatest football players of all time.
The English Premier League is one of the most watched football leagues in the world.
Real Madrid has won the UEFA Champions League a record 15 times, as of 2024.
The UEFA Champions League is Europe's top club football competition, held annually since 1955, originally as the European Cup.
A red card results in a player being sent off, and their team plays with one fewer player for the rest of the match.
A yellow card is a caution; receiving two yellow cards in a match results in a red card and ejection.
The Ballon d'Or is an annual award given to the best football player in the world.
FC Barcelona and Real Madrid share one of the most famous rivalries in football, known as El Clasico.
The 1994 FIFA World Cup, held in the United States, remains the most attended World Cup in history.
Football is governed internationally by FIFA, the Federation Internationale de Football Association, founded in 1904.
The FIFA Women's World Cup was first held in 1991 and was won by the United States.
The fastest goal in World Cup history was scored by Hakan Sukur of Turkey, just 11 seconds into a match in 2002.
A professional football pitch is typically 100 to 110 meters long and 64 to 75 meters wide.
Penalty shootouts are used to decide knockout matches that remain tied after extra time.
The current FIFA World Cup trophy has been awarded to the winning nation since 1974.
